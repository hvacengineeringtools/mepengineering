2. Tower characteristic

How a tower’s performance changes with flow depends on its characteristic KaV/L against L/G. That is a property of the fill, the tower type and its size, and it is not on any nameplate. The handbook line KaV/L = C·(L/G)−m is a worked sample for an open-circuit tower with fill; it is not general. Choose how to give the characteristic, preferably from the maker’s data. Closed-circuit towers, dry or hybrid coolers and evaporative condensers do not follow the Merkel method and are not covered.

    Merkel integral and tower characteristic

      Water use: evaporation, bleed-off, drift and make-up

        Empirical form from M374 §8 (after Perry’s Handbook): about 1.8 m³ evaporates for every 10 000 000 kcal rejected, which is 0.00153 m³ per m³ circulated per K of range. The rule of thumb in §20 is 0.1 % of the circulating flow per °F of range (0.18 % per K). The heat balance uses the share of the heat that leaves as vapour, 60 to 95 % (§20), divided by the latent heat of water (about 2430 kJ/kg near 30 °C). Blow-down, drift and make-up: M374 §8, §10 and example 15-1.

        Adjustment, §6.4.1.2.1

          Part-load value and the maker’s selection

            Section 5.2 scales the maker’s COP and IPLV by the ratio of the 90.1 factors at your conditions and at the maker’s conditions. 90.1 applies Kadj to the IPLV requirement in the same way, so an adjusted IPLV is the standard’s NPLV. The factor is a curve for the minimum requirement; it is not the maker’s data for the unit, and it ignores any change in capacity, flow or compressor limits. Ask the maker for a selection at the new conditions and enter it in step 3 to see the difference.

            Worked with the values on the sheet

            What each 90.1-2025 (SI) table assumes when it states a minimum efficiency: the chilled water, the heat-rejection side, the outdoor air and the part-load points. “Standard” conditions are those of AHRI 551/591 (SI), which Table 6.8.1-3 and 6.8.1-16 refer to. The tables in 90.1 give the requirement; the conditions behind IPLV.SI are in AHRI 551/591 (SI) Tables 4 and 6, not in 90.1 itself.

            Chillers, Tables 6.8.1-3 and 6.8.1-16

            Full-load rating: 7.00 °C leaving and 12.00 °C entering chilled water (§6.4.1.2.1). Water-cooled condenser: 30.00 °C entering and 35.00 °C leaving. Air-cooled: 35.0 °C entering dry-bulb. IPLV.SI = 0.01A + 0.42B + 0.45C + 0.12D, where A, B, C, D are the COPs at 100, 75, 50 and 25 % load. In AHRI 551/591 the condenser water flow is held at its full-load value at every part-load point, and the chilled water leaves at 7.00 °C throughout.

            Part-load points of the IPLV.SI rating

            At part load the heat-rejection temperature falls with the load, which is why IPLV.SI is higher than the full-load COP. Water-cooled units are rated with condenser water falling from 30.0 to 19.0 °C (it stops falling at 19.0 °C between 50 and 25 % load); air-cooled units with ambient falling from 35.0 to 13.0 °C. A plant whose tower or ambient does not follow that pattern will not see the IPLV.SI in service.

            Heat-rejection equipment, Table 6.8.1-7

            These describe the tower, not the chiller. An open-circuit tower is rated at 35.0 °C water in, 29.4 °C water out and 23.9 °C entering wet-bulb: a range of 5.6 K and an approach of 5.5 K. The chiller standard condition of 30.00 / 35.00 °C is a range of 5.0 K, and its 30.00 °C entering water is 0.6 K warmer than the tower’s rating outlet. The two ratings are separate. Your site’s wet-bulb, tower approach and chiller lift decide the real conditions.

            What distinguishes the unit types

            • Air-cooled: rated on outdoor dry-bulb (35.0 °C). The condenser leaving temperature does not exist as a water condition, so no Kadj applies. Ambient is the variable that changes performance.
            • Liquid-cooled, positive displacement and absorption: rated at 30.00 / 35.00 °C condenser water. No Kadj; other water temperatures need the maker’s data.
            • Liquid-cooled centrifugal: the same standard point, but 90.1 allows the requirement to be adjusted by Kadj for a design outside it, between 2.20 and 21.10 °C leaving evaporator water, 15.56 to 57.00 °C leaving condenser water and a lift of 11.00 to 44.00 K.
            • Heat pumps and heat recovery (Table 6.8.1-16): the cooling rating is the same as above. Heating has its own rating points: air-source 8.00 °C db / 6.00 °C wb and −8.00 °C db / −9.00 °C wb; liquid-source with the source water leaving at 7.00 °C (19.00 °C for the boost point), and heating water at 35/40, 42/50, 50/60 °C (low, medium, high) or 50/60 °C (boost).
            • Evaporatively cooled chillers: AHRI 551/591 rates them on 24.00 °C entering wet-bulb (part load 24.0, 20.5, 17.0, 13.5 °C). They are not a row in Table 6.8.1-3.

            Tower method

            Range, approach, effectiveness, evaporation and blow-down are as in PDH course M374 (Primo), sections 6 to 8, 15 and 20. The tower characteristic is the Merkel equation evaluated with four points at 10, 40, 60 and 90 % of the range (the usual Cooling Technology Institute four-point integration). Air leaving the fill is taken at an enthalpy rise of (L/G)·cw·ΔT, as in the handbook, which neglects the small evaporation term. The figures were checked against the handbook’s worked examples 15-2 to 15-7 and agree within 0.02 °F.

            Existing tower in part 1

            With the box ticked, part 1 treats the tower as a fixed machine. Its characteristic (part 2) is frozen at the operating point present when the box was ticked or the characteristic last changed. Only three boxes are then free inputs, because the tower itself decides the approach: approach = f(range, flow, wet-bulb). Change the flow while holding wet-bulb and heat rejected, and the approach, range and both water temperatures all move, matching the result of part 3. Untick the box for plain arithmetic between the boxes (R = H − C, A = C − W, Q = 4.186·flow·R), where holding the wet-bulb, approach and load fixes everything except range and flow.

            The characteristic is not universal

            The line KaV/L = C·(L/G)−m in the handbook is a worked sample for an open-circuit tower with fill; the slope and constant differ between film and splash fill, counterflow and crossflow, and between makers. Use the maker’s constants, or two points read from the maker’s curve, whenever you have them. The sheet then finds the air flow that puts the operating point on that curve. Without them the sheet gives an estimate: it passes the line through the operating point with an assumed L/G and slope. Closed-circuit towers, dry and hybrid coolers and evaporative condensers have other physics and are not covered by this sheet.

            What is assumed

            The characteristic keeps the same constant and slope at other flows. The air flow of the first case is fixed unless the fan percentage is changed; it is not recalculated for the higher fill pressure drop at a higher flow, which the handbook example also ignores. Water is 1 kg per litre with cw = 4.186 kJ/(kg·K). Hot-air recirculation, wet-bulb rise at the tower inlet and bypass wall water (handbook section 16) are not modelled.

            How much does the unknown L/G matter? (estimate mode)

            Little for a flow change at constant load. Varying the assumed L/G from 0.7 to 1.8 moves the new water-out temperature by about 0.1 to 0.3 K; the slope m matters about as much. The sheet prints the spread for L/G 0.7 to 1.6 and m 0.5 to 0.8 under the result. For a firm answer use the maker’s characteristic or performance curve at the new flow.

            Approach limit

            The handbook notes that makers rarely guarantee less than about 2.8 K approach. The sheet marks anything below that.

            Chiller

            §6.4.1.2.1: Kadj applies to liquid-cooled centrifugal chillers not designed for the AHRI 551/591 standard conditions (7.00 °C leaving and 12.00 °C entering evaporator water, 30.00 °C entering and 35.00 °C leaving condenser water). At exactly 7.00 and 35.00 °C the sheet applies no adjustment; the formula gives 0.9995. The adjusted values are valid only for LvgEvap 2.20 to 21.10 °C, LvgCond 15.56 to 57.00 °C and lift 11.00 to 44.00 K; outside them the standard does not cover the chiller. Other types in Table 6.8.1-3 have no adjustment: they are rated at the test standard’s conditions.

            The standard’s text calls the adjusted figures “maximum”; they are minimum COP values.

            Water use

            Three evaporation estimates are shown side by side. They normally agree within about 15 %. The empirical formula and the heat balance (at about 89 % evaporative share) are the same line; the rule of thumb of 0.1 % per °F is the more conservative. Bleed-off is set by the cycles of concentration: B = E/(N − 1). Make-up is evaporation plus bleed-off plus drift. Cycles of concentration depend on the make-up water quality and treatment, and the conductivity ratio gives it directly where you can measure both waters. The sheet does not model the variation of evaporation with the dry-bulb temperature and humidity of the air, nor water used for filter backwash or basin overflow.

            Drift loss: what is acceptable

            Drift is water carried out as droplets, as a percentage of the circulating flow. In normal design:

            • 0.002 % or less: good, and the usual specification for a new tower with high-efficiency drift eliminators. This is the current Eurovent certification threshold.
            • 0.005 %: a common maker’s guarantee for conventional eliminators.
            • 0.01 %: the upper limit of acceptable practice today. It was the previous Eurovent threshold.
            • 0.02 %: the older specification quoted in M374 §10, now regarded as poor. Modern eliminators reach 0.001 to 0.003 %.

            Drift matters for water loss (small) and for Legionella control and nearby plant, where the droplets can carry bacteria and chemicals off the site. The figure is a maker’s guarantee, tested to a standard such as CTI ATC-140 or the Eurovent programme, and holds only while the eliminators are clean and undamaged and the tower runs near its rated air velocity. The sheet marks anything above 0.01 % as high. Check the limit in your project specification or local code: the Hong Kong EMSD code of practice for water-cooled air-conditioning systems gives no numeric drift limit, but requires eliminators to be maintained, and sets a minimum of 6 cycles of concentration for fresh water and 2 for sea water (section 2.4.2).

            Manufacturer’s selection (section 5.2)

            Enter the maker’s selection at its rated water temperatures and change “your conditions” in section 5. The sheet estimates the COP, input power and IPLV at your conditions from the 90.1 factor, shows the change, and checks both the maker’s figures and the estimate against the 90.1 minimum at the corresponding conditions. The matrix underneath gives the estimated COP for a range of condenser and evaporator leaving temperatures. Only the leaving temperatures enter Kadj; the entering temperatures give the temperature differences and the chilled-water flow. The factor applies to liquid-cooled centrifugal units; for other types, enter the maker’s own figures at your conditions in step 3.

            Unit already selected

            If the maker’s COP is quoted at the same water temperatures as the new conditions, compare it directly with the adjusted requirement. If the conditions changed after the unit was rated, the sheet estimates the unit’s new COP as COP × Kadj(new) ÷ Kadj(rated). Kadj is a curve for the minimum requirement and is not the maker’s data for the unit, so treat that as a guide.

            Points to check in the standard’s tables

            Table 6.8.1-3 gives the last positive-displacement size as “≥2100 kW” while the row above ends at 2110 kW; the sheet uses 2110 kW. Table 6.8.1-16 prints the IPLV.SI for liquid-source positive displacement, 1055 to 2110 kW, as “≤6.432” and “≤8.157”; every other row is “≥”, so the sheet treats them as minimums. The first liquid-source centrifugal row prints “<39.57 and <528”; the sheet reads it as 39.57 to 528 kW (note q).
