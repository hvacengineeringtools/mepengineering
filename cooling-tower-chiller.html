    Water use: evaporation, bleed-off, drift and make-up

      Empirical form from M374 §8 (after Perry’s Handbook): about 1.8 m³ evaporates for every 10 000 000 kcal rejected, which is 0.00153 m³ per m³ circulated per K of range. The rule of thumb in §20 is 0.1 % of the circulating flow per °F of range (0.18 % per K). The heat balance uses the share of the heat that leaves as vapour, 60 to 95 % (§20), divided by the latent heat of water (about 2430 kJ/kg near 30 °C). Blow-down, drift and make-up: M374 §8, §10 and example 15-1.

      Heat-rejection equipment, Table 6.8.1-7

      These describe the tower, not the chiller. An open-circuit tower is rated at 35.0 °C water in, 29.4 °C water out and 23.9 °C entering wet-bulb: a range of 5.6 K and an approach of 5.5 K. The chiller standard condition of 30.00 / 35.00 °C is a range of 5.0 K, and its 30.00 °C entering water is 0.6 K warmer than the tower’s rating outlet. The two ratings are separate. Your site’s wet-bulb, tower approach and chiller lift decide the real conditions.

      Open-circuit tower performance is the rated water flow at the thermal rating condition divided by the fan motor nameplate power (note a). Closed-circuit includes the spray pump (note b). The requirement does not apply to field-erected towers (note e).

      Method

      Heat rejected = water flow × 4.186 × range, with water at 1 kg per litre. Range = water in − water out; approach = water out − wet-bulb; effectiveness = range ÷ (range + approach). Evaporation, blow-down, drift and make-up follow PDH course M374 (Primo), sections 6 to 8, 10 and 20. When you change the flow or load, the new range follows from the heat balance, and the water temperatures follow from whichever values you hold. They are not predicted from the tower’s own response.

      What this sheet does not do

      It does not model the tower’s own response (the Merkel method), so it cannot say how much water out rises when the flow rises, how the approach changes with the wet-bulb, or what the air flow does. That needs the tower characteristic (KaV/L against L/G) from the maker, or the maker’s performance curve. Closed-circuit towers, dry coolers and evaporative condensers are not covered.

      Approach limit

      The handbook notes that makers rarely guarantee less than about 2.8 K approach. The sheet marks anything below that.

      Water use

      Three evaporation estimates are shown side by side. They normally agree within about 15 %. The empirical formula and the heat balance (at about 89 % evaporative share) are the same line; the rule of thumb of 0.1 % per °F is the more conservative. Bleed-off is set by the cycles of concentration: B = E/(N − 1). Make-up is evaporation plus bleed-off plus drift. Cycles of concentration depend on the make-up water quality and treatment, and the conductivity ratio gives it directly where you can measure both waters. The sheet does not model the variation of evaporation with the dry-bulb temperature and humidity of the air, nor water used for filter backwash or basin overflow.

      Drift loss: what is acceptable

      Drift is water carried out as droplets, as a percentage of the circulating flow. In normal design:

      • 0.002 % or less: good, and the usual specification for a new tower with high-efficiency drift eliminators. This is the current Eurovent certification threshold.
      • 0.005 %: a common maker’s guarantee for conventional eliminators.
      • 0.01 %: the upper limit of acceptable practice today. It was the previous Eurovent threshold.
      • 0.02 %: the older specification quoted in M374 §10, now regarded as poor. Modern eliminators reach 0.001 to 0.003 %.

      Drift matters for water loss (small) and for Legionella control and nearby plant, where the droplets can carry bacteria and chemicals off the site. The figure is a maker’s guarantee, tested to a standard such as CTI ATC-140 or the Eurovent programme, and holds only while the eliminators are clean and undamaged and the tower runs near its rated air velocity. The sheet marks anything above 0.01 % as high. Check the limit in your project specification or local code: the Hong Kong EMSD code of practice for water-cooled air-conditioning systems gives no numeric drift limit, but requires eliminators to be maintained, and sets a minimum of 6 cycles of concentration for fresh water and 2 for sea water (section 2.4.2).
