Caution · A3, higher flammability
Manufacturer may have specific recommendations for ventilation and gas sensors

This refrigerant is a hydrocarbon and is highly flammable. The airflows below are a starting point only. Before sizing the fan, check the equipment maker’s installation instructions for:

  • the ventilation rate, and where the extract and make-up air must be;
  • the gas sensor: type, number, height (low, as the gas is heavier than air) and alarm set point;
  • fans, lights and electrics suitable for a flammable atmosphere;
  • charge limits for an A3 refrigerant, which this sheet does not check.

Where the maker asks for more than this sheet, follow the maker.

Warning · R717 ammonia · B2L, higher toxicity
Ammonia — not recommended. Detailed checking is needed.

Ammonia is toxic at very low concentrations, and ASHRAE 15 no longer covers it. Do not size an ammonia plant room from this sheet alone: the figures below follow IIAR 2 in outline and are for reference only.

  • A specialist design to IIAR 2 and BS EN 378 is needed.
  • It must cover detection and alarm levels, emergency ventilation, temperature control, the discharge point and the room’s construction and exits.
  • Ammonia is lighter than air: detectors and extract go high.

?Why ASHRAE 15 gives a much larger airflow for A2L and B2L
  1. It is sized for a different purpose.For A1, B1 and the other groups, Level 2 is Equation 8-1b, a general purge rate that depends only on the charge. A2L and B2L refrigerants are mildly flammable, so for them Level 2 is sized to dilute the refrigerant from a large leak and keep the room below the concentration at which it could ignite.
  2. It follows the leak, not only the charge.How fast refrigerant escapes depends on the system’s design pressure, so a higher pressure gives a higher airflow. The rate grows with charge up to the threshold charge G′; above G′ it levels off at the plateau rate Q′.
  3. A bigger room does not reduce it.The Level 2 airflow does not depend on room volume. A larger room gives fewer air changes an hour, but the same airflow.
  4. BS EN 378 works differently.Its machinery-room formula, like Equation 8-1b, depends only on the charge, so it gives much less air. The two standards are not interchangeable; design to the one that applies to the project.
  5. How to bring it down.Split the charge into smaller independent circuits, since G is the charge of the largest single circuit, or choose equipment with a lower design pressure.

B2L refrigerants other than ammonia use the same method. Ammonia follows IIAR 2.

Use of this sheet

Preliminary check only. Confirm each clause against a licensed copy before the airflow is used for a fan. National rules can ask for more than the minimum of 4 air changes an hour. This sheet does not place the detector, the exhaust inlet or the discharge.

BS EN 378-3, machinery room

Clause 5.13. Qmin is 4 air changes an hour, the continuous minimum. Qmax is 0.014 × charge2/3 m³/s. Fifteen air changes an hour is taken as always sufficient, so it caps Qmax.

BS EN 378-2, ventilated enclosure

Clause 6.2.15. Qmin by calculation is compared with 4 air changes an hour and with 2 m³/h; the greatest is used. The enclosure is kept at 20 Pa or more below the room. Charge limits from toxicity and flammability are not assessed here. The clause is written for A2L, B2L, A2, B2, A3 and B3; for A1 and B1 the number is still shown.

ASHRAE 15

Level 1 is the greater of 0.5 cfm/ft² of floor (2.54 l/s per m²) and 20 cfm (9.44 l/s) a person. Level 1 must also be able to hold the room within a 10 °C temperature rise and below 50 °C; the sheet does not check this. Level 2 for groups other than A2L and B2L is Q = 70 × √charge l/s (Equation 8-1b).

For A2L and B2L other than R-717, Level 2 follows Table 8-3, the calculation method that stands as an alternative to the chart. With P the absolute design pressure: G′ = 267 × P−0.72 kg and Q′ = 6.67 × P0.62 m³/s. Below 0.1 G′ the rate is 0.102 Q′; between 0.1 G′ and G′ it is Q′ × [1 + 0.39 × ln(G/G′)]; above G′ it is Q′. It is never less than Level 1 and is rounded up to two significant figures. The SI coefficient 6.67 is from the June 2024 errata to ASHRAE 15-2022; the printed 0.400 was wrong. The chart, but not the equations, stops at 4.8 MPa design pressure.

The A2L rate is sized to keep a large leak of a flammable refrigerant diluted, so it is usually far above the Equation 8-1b rate and the BS EN 378 rate. Several hundred air changes an hour is normal for a large charge in a small room. To reduce it, split the charge into smaller independent circuits, or lower the design pressure if the equipment allows. A larger room does not reduce the Level 2 airflow, which does not depend on room volume.

Addendum q to ASHRAE 15-2022 (2024) takes R-717 out of the scope of ASHRAE 15; ammonia machinery rooms follow IIAR 2. For R-717 the sheet therefore uses IIAR 2-2021: occupied ventilation the greater of 0.5 cfm/ft² (2.54 l/s per m²) and 20 cfm (9.44 l/s) a person; emergency ventilation 30 air changes an hour of the gross room volume, started by the detector at 150 ppm; and temperature-control ventilation to keep the room at or below 40 °C (104 °F), which the sheet does not calculate. The 30 air-change rate is meant to keep a large release below ammonia’s flammable limit. Ammonia is toxic long before it is flammable, so detection, alarm, evacuation and shutdown carry the safety case. IIAR 2 also asks for an emergency-fan discharge velocity of at least 2,500 ft/min (12.7 m/s).

High-side design pressure

The pressure the high side (compressor discharge, condenser, receiver and liquid line) is designed and tested for, as a gauge pressure. On European equipment it is PS on the nameplate. The sheet fills a typical value: the refrigerant’s saturation pressure, at the bubble point for blends, at the condensing temperature BS EN 378-2 specifies for an air-cooled condenser at the chosen ambient. Pressures are calculated with CoolProp; blend values use its mixture model. For water-cooled plant, BS EN 378-2 uses the maximum leaving water temperature plus 8 K instead. Choose “Other condensing temperature” to use your own temperature from 20 to 75 °C, or “Type the pressure” to enter any value. Typing in the pressure box switches to “Type the pressure”, so a later change of refrigerant keeps your figure. Always check against the PS on the nameplate. R744 has no saturation pressure above 31 °C, so type its PS. The same pressures are listed in the refrigerant data table.

Outdoor opening: natural ventilation

The outdoor opening is a natural-ventilation requirement. It applies only where the refrigerating system is outdoors, more than 6.1 m (20 ft) from any building opening, and enclosed by a penthouse, lean-to or other open structure. Such an enclosure may be ventilated naturally or mechanically. For natural ventilation the free opening area is F = 0.138 × √G m² (F = G0.5 ft², G in lb). If mechanical ventilation is used instead, size the fan as for a machinery room. Place the openings by the refrigerant’s density relative to air: low for refrigerants heavier than air, high for lighter ones such as R-717. It does not apply to an indoor machinery room, which needs the Level 1 and Level 2 fans.
