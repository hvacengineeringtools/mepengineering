Other items in the airway

A pressure drop only means something at a velocity. It rises with the square of the air speed, so 4 Pa at 2.0 m/s becomes 6.3 Pa at 2.5 m/s. For each item, enter the maker’s figure and the velocity it was quoted at; the sheet converts it to this job.

  • Figure already at your airflow and size, for example from the maker’s selection software: leave the velocity blank. It is added as typed.
  • Item the same size as the louvre, for example a damper fitted directly behind it: leave the size blank. The sheet uses the louvre face velocity.
  • Item smaller or larger than the louvre, for example a fire damper in the duct: give its size. The sheet uses the air speed through the item, not the louvre face velocity.
  • Not fitted: leave the row blank.

Use of this sheet

Preliminary sizing only. Use the maker’s stated coefficient and free area, and the area that coefficient was measured on. Confirm the airflow and rain classes on the BS EN 13030 test for that louvre. This sheet does not calculate sound reduction.

Coefficient basis

BS EN 13030 measures Ce and Cd on the core area: the face of the louvre less its frame. Free area is not used in the test. A core-area coefficient applied to the free area overstates the pressure drop by 1/R², four times at 50% free area.

Face area and opening size

The face area here is the core area. The overall louvre, and the builder’s opening, are larger by the frame and any mullions. Take those from the maker.

Rain class

The rain test runs with a 13 m/s wind at face velocities from 0 to 3.5 m/s. A louvre can be class A at a low velocity and class C at a higher one, so read the class at the design face velocity.

Air density

Pressure drop is proportional to air density. The sheet works it out from the air temperature and the site elevation. Atmospheric pressure comes from the standard-atmosphere equation in ASHRAE Fundamentals, Chapter 1; density then follows from the ideal-gas law for dry air. Humidity lowers density slightly, usually by less than 1%. For example, exhaust air at 35 °C is about 1.15 kg/m³ at sea level, about 5% below standard air at 20 °C.

Other items in the airway

Mesh, dampers, attenuators and plenums add their own pressure drop to the louvre’s. Enter each from the maker’s data with the face velocity it was quoted at. Pressure drop rises with the square of velocity, so the sheet scales each figure by (velocity through the item ÷ quoted velocity)². That velocity is the louvre face velocity unless you give the item its own size, as for a fire damper in a smaller duct. It also scales by density relative to standard air at 1.2 kg/m³, the usual basis for makers’ data. With no quoted velocity, the figure is added as typed. Insect mesh can add a noticeable pressure drop; check it is in the maker’s louvre test or add it here, not both.

Calculating the mesh

Without maker’s data, tick “Calculate the mesh pressure drop from the mesh size”. For a square woven mesh with aperture a and wire diameter d, the open area is f = [a / (a + d)]², or type the maker’s open area. The loss coefficient for a wire screen is ζ = 1.3 × (1 − f) + (1/f − 1)² (Idelchik and Fried, Flow Resistance: A Design Guide for Engineers, 1989). The pressure drop is ζ × ½ρV², with V the louvre face velocity, assuming the mesh covers the whole core.

Mesh sizes

No standard fixes the mesh fitted behind a louvre; it is the maker’s choice or the specifier’s. Woven wire cloth is made to ISO 9044, and ISO 4783 lists preferred combinations of aperture and wire diameter. The Mesh type list gives common louvre meshes as a starting point: 12–19 mm square bird mesh in 1.0–1.6 mm wire, 6 mm vermin mesh, and 1–2 mm insect mesh. Every value can be changed. A preset gives these open areas:

MeshAperture a, mmWire d, mmOpen area fζ

Insect mesh roughly doubles to quadruples the pressure drop of a bird mesh at the same face velocity, and clogs. Where insect mesh is required, consider a larger louvre or a removable screen.

The formula is for a clean mesh and a Reynolds number of about 1,000 or more. The sheet works the Reynolds number out from the wire diameter and the speed through the openings. Fine insect meshes at louvre speeds often fall below 1,000, where the real loss is higher, so the result is then a minimum. Mesh also collects dirt and insects; plan for cleaning or allow a margin.

Marked results

A blade velocity of 5 m/s or more, and a pressure drop above 150 Pa, are shaded as prompts to check the selection. They are this sheet’s own prompts, not limits from a standard.

How the air feels

The drawing is original to this site. Up to 1.5 m/s it follows the ASHRAE table of occupant reaction in a room; above that, the Beaufort effects on people after Penwarden (1973). Outdoor wind is gusty and a louvre jet is steadier, so treat the drawing as a guide. Near an intake the air slows quickly with distance from the face.
