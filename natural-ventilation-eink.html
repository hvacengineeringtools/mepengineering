1. Opening

Air enters and leaves through the same opening in one wall. The driving force is the indoor–outdoor temperature difference.

2. Formula

Q = Cd · (A / 3) · √(Δθ · g · H2 / θ̄)
with opening light, multiply by J(φ)

Cd is usually 0.61. Δθ is |Ti − To|. θ̄ is the mean absolute temperature.

3. Opening light

An opening light is a casement or pivot pane that does not open to the full free area. If the hole is unobstructed, choose No.

How to find J(φ)

  1. Choose the window type: side-mounted casement, or centre-pivoted.
  2. Measure the opening angle φ. Closed = 0°. Fully open = 90°.
  3. Work out the opening-light height ÷ width ratio.
  4. Read J(φ) from fig. 3a or 3b, or use the lookup underneath.
  5. The value is applied automatically in the calculation.

Figs. 3a and 3b plot the J(φ) values this sheet uses. They were read from the opening-light charts of BS 5925:1991 at 0, 15, 30, 45, 60 and 90° and joined with straight lines. Check them against your copy of the standard. Sheets II and III use the same curves for each opening.

5. Temperature sweep

Indoor temperature held still; outdoor temperature moves ±10 °C.

Method: BS 5925:1991, single opening, buoyancy only. Wind, cross-ventilation and multi-opening flow are not included. g = 9.81 m/s²; mean absolute temperature θ̄ = (Ti + To)/2 + 273.15 K. J(φ) is interpolated linearly between values read from the BS 5925 opening-light charts at 0, 15, 30, 45, 60 and 90°, for the three height-to-width ratios shown; confirm against the standard. The temperature sweep holds Ti and varies To from Ti − 10 K to Ti + 10 K.
