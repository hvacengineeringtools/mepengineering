Sizes the feed and expansion tank of a water system open to the air. Enter the total water volume of the system; the sheet adds a safety factor, finds how much the water expands between the lowest and highest temperature, and sets the tank at twice that expansion so it is never more than half full. · ·

        The calculation step by step, with the values now on the sheet. The figures update as you type.

        What the method does

        The water in the system expands when it warms from t1 to t2. The expansion goes up into the open tank. The tank is made twice that volume, so that it holds the expansion plus the same amount again as a reserve and the float valve still has a level to work with. The pipe also grows with temperature, which makes the pipe hold slightly more water; the term 3α(t2 − t1) takes that off.

        Compared with the ME1516 sheet

        • The sheet leaves the pipe expansion coefficient blank, so its pipe-expansion term is zero. Enter 99.13 m³ with 20 %, choose “Ignore pipe expansion” and “Rounded to 6 decimals” to reproduce its result: 118 956 l and 1189.56 l for the tank.
        • With the specific volumes calculated from temperature (0.0010001 and 0.0010053 m³/kg at 7 and 33 °C) the expansion is 0.52 % rather than the 0.50 % that the rounded values give. The rounding to 6 decimals matters here because the whole answer comes from a difference in the fourth significant figure.
        • The sheet lists P1 = 170 kPa, P2 = 270 kPa and atmospheric pressure, but the open-tank formula does not use them. They would matter only for a closed (pressurised) expansion vessel, which is not covered here.
        • The sheet notes “say 1000 l” beside 1189.56 l. A tank cannot be smaller than the calculated capacity, so the next standard size above it is needed.

        Limits

        • The volume is whatever you enter. Include the water in pipe, chillers, coils, heat exchangers and buffer vessels, but not the tank itself. Pipe volume is area × length, with the inside diameter, summed over every size.
        • The temperature rise is taken from the lowest temperature of the water to the highest. A system that is drained and refilled in winter, or a heating system, needs the right pair of temperatures.
        • Glycol or other additives change the density; the specific volume of plain water is used.
        • The tank must also be above the highest point of the system by the head the designer needs, and the make-up arrangement, overflow and venting are not sized here.
        • The “standard size” list is generic round volumes for rough selection, not a maker’s catalogue.
