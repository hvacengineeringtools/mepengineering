Notes and formulae

The working with the values now on the sheet.

1. One load

Given kVA: kW = kVA × pf. Given kW: kVA = kW ÷ pf. Area schedule: kVA = Σ(area × VA/m² ÷ 1000) × spare, kW = kVA × pf.
Starting kVA = kVA × starting factor.
A motor load adds its starting kVA to the motor total. A non-motor load adds its kW to the steady total.

2. Peak motor load

Pmotor = peak motor factor × Σ motor starting kVA, in kW. The factor is 0.75 (motors starting together) × 0.65 (starting power factor) = 0.4875. Some designers round the product to 0.5.

3. Transient load

Ptransient = Pmotor + Σ non-motor kW.

4. Minimum genset rating

kVAmin = Ptransient ÷ overload ÷ power factor ÷ step-load factor
Typical allowances: overload 1.1, power factor 0.8, step-load factor 0.65 to 0.75.

5. Nominal rating

The smallest standard size not less than kVAmin.

The factor is the starting kVA divided by the running kVA. The starting kVA of a motor is its locked-rotor kVA, which depends on the starter. The values are typical; where the maker gives the locked-rotor kVA or the code letter of the motor, use that.

How the methods relate

Direct on line (DOL) is the reference: a standard induction motor draws about 6 to 8 times its full-load current, so 7 is a common figure.
Star-delta (Y/D) starts on one third of the voltage, which cuts the current to one third: 7 ÷ 3 ≈ 2.33.
An auto-transformer starter gives the factor of the DOL start times the tap setting squared: 65 % tap → 7 × 0.65² ≈ 2.96, 80 % tap → 4.5, 50 % tap → 1.75.
A soft starter limits the current, often to about 3 times full load.
A variable-speed drive (inverter) starts at close to full-load current.
ACVV is the variable-voltage, variable-frequency drive of a lift. Values from about 1.7 up to 2.5 are used; take the higher one when the lift maker has not given a figure.
Non-motor and electronic loads (lighting, small power, servers, heaters) have no starting surge: use 1.

Choosing a method on a load

Pick the method on the load row. If it is not yet in the table in section 2, it is added there with its typical factor, which you can then change. Changing a factor in the table changes every load that uses the method. A load with the method Static is set to count as non-motor; any other method sets it to count as motor. Change “Counts as” if the load differs.

Per-load power factor

Pumps and lifts are often taken at 0.85, lighting at 0.9 and static loads at 0.8. Where kW is given, kVA = kW ÷ efficiency ÷ power factor, for example 0.85 × 0.8 = 0.68. Set the factor on each row.

Loads that start but count as steady

A load can have a motor starter but still be counted in the steady total, for example a CRAC unit that runs all the time. Set “Counts as” to choose which total a row goes into.

What is not included

Voltage dip, harmonics, derating for altitude and temperature, sequencing of the starts, and the manufacturer’s transient data. The step-load acceptance factor should be confirmed with the manufacturer.
