    How to fill in Iac (interior shade)
    What it isThe interior attenuation coefficient: the share of the solar gain that still gets through once an interior shading device (blind, roller shade, curtain) is fitted over the glass. Gain = area × SHGC × Iac × Et.
    No shadeEnter 1. This is the default and must stay 1 when nothing is fitted, or when the glass is bare at the time you are checking.
    With a shadeEnter the value for that device on that glazing. Take it from the ASHRAE Handbook Fundamentals fenestration chapter (the IAC tables for drapes, roller shades and venetian blinds), or from the shade manufacturer's data sheet. The value depends on the shade type, its colour and openness, the slat angle for blinds, and the glass behind it. Lighter and more reflective shades give lower numbers.
    RangeBetween 0 and 1. A value above 1 is not possible; the box stops at 1.
    Do not double countIf the SHGC you entered already includes a shade (a blind between the panes, or a manufacturer figure quoted "with shade"), keep Iac at 1.
    Not covered hereOutside shading — overhangs, fins, external louvres and neighbouring buildings. Iac is for interior devices only. Reduce Et separately for outside shade, or leave the result as an upper bound.
    Blind open or closedOne Iac applies to one condition. For a design check, use the shade position you expect at peak sun, and run the sheet again with 1 for the bare-glass worst case.
    Typical SHGC and Iac values, and what they assume

    Indicative values to start from when you have no product data. They are centre-of-glass figures for unshaded glazing, which is what the ASHRAE simplified method uses with the glazed area. A manufacturer's data sheet for the actual product always replaces them.

    Whole-window SHGC (frame included) is lower than the centre-of-glass figure. If you enter a whole-window SHGC, use the whole opening area instead of the glazed area, never both.

    How to fill in τb and τd
    Where to get itOpen ASHRAE climatic design conditions (ashrae-meteo.info) and choose the station nearest to the site (similar climate and elevation), by name or on the map. Pick the edition of the climatic data you are working to (2009, 2013, 2017, 2021 or later). In the "Monthly Design Conditions" table, under "Clear Sky Solar Irradiance", copy the rows named taub and taud. These are ASHRAE's names for τb (beam) and τd (diffuse); they also appear in Handbook Fundamentals Chapter 14. Table layout differs between editions, so match the row names, not the table number.
    How many, in what orderExactly 12 entries in each box: January first, December last, in calendar order whatever the hemisphere (only the blank-box estimate is shifted by six months for the south). Put a dash (-) for any month you do not have: that month uses the sheet's estimate.
    How to pasteCopy the row (or the 12-cell column) and paste it as it is. Commas, spaces, tabs, semicolons and new lines are all accepted. Use a decimal point; no units, month names or header text.
    Which boxτb values go in the first box and τd in the second. τd is several times larger than τb at every station, so if the first box holds the bigger numbers the columns are swapped.
    CheckEach counter must read 12 values, and the line under the boxes shows the τb and τd used for the chosen date. Compare your values with the estimate table below: the same order of size means a sensible entry; a value far outside means a wrong row or a typing slip.
    Blank or partialEither box may stay empty and then uses the sheet's generic estimate from month and elevation, which is for a rough check only. When you do fill them, use both boxes from the same station: a station τb with an estimated τd is a pair the model was never fitted to.

    5. Walls and the horizontal on the chosen date

    Clear-sky irradiance Et on vertical walls facing each way, and on the horizontal, before any glass (no SHGC or Iac). Walls include beam, diffuse and ground reflection; the horizontal is beam on the flat plane plus diffuse. For the annual maximum on each wall, see section 3 with the tilt at 90°.

    Hour by hour

    Local clock time, W/m². Only hours with the sun up are listed. A low value on a wall means it is in shade for the beam and receives only diffuse and ground reflection.

    6. Windows in the area: worst glass gain

    List every window of the room or zone. The sheet adds them up at each moment of the year with the place, ground albedo and optical depths in section 1, and finds the highest total. That coincident total is the worst glass gain for the area. Each row has its own size, facing, tilt, glass and shade. Sections 2 to 4 stay as the single-window check.

    Worst glass gain, definition. The highest total solar heat gain through all the listed windows at one single moment of the year:
    Qworst = max over time t of Σi Ai × SHGCi × Iac,i × Et,i(t)  [kW]
    where i is each window row, Ai its glass area (width × height × number), and Et,i(t) the clear-sky irradiance (beam + diffuse + ground-reflected) on that window's own facing and tilt at time t. The sheet adds the windows together at each moment, checks every day of the year at 6-minute steps over the full 24 hours, refines the highest to 36 seconds, and reports that total with its date and time. It is a peak solar gain through the glass, not a cooling load.

    Clear-sky method of ASHRAE Handbook—Fundamentals 2025, Chapter 14, with the simplified fenestration relation q = A × Et × SHGC × Iac of Chapter 15. Azimuth is entered as a compass bearing and converted to the Handbook's surface azimuth measured from south. Clock time is local, with daylight saving taken from the time zone. Every yearly scan runs over the full 24 hours of every day at 6-minute steps and then refines to 36 seconds. Section 6 sums A × SHGC × Iac × Et of every listed window at the same moment and reports the highest coincident total; each window is also scanned on its own for comparison. Horizontal windows take no ground reflection. Diffuse irradiance on the glass uses the Handbook relation: Ed·Y on a vertical surface, with Y = max(0.45, 0.55 + 0.437 cos θ + 0.313 cos² θ), and Ed(Y sin Σ + cos Σ) on a tilted surface (Ed Y sin Σ when Σ exceeds 90°). The solar constant is taken as 1361.1 W/m². Results are instantaneous clear-sky glass gain, an upper bound, not the cooling load: stored heat in the room delays and lowers the peak the cooling system sees. SHGC is a normal-incidence value, so gain at beam incidence angles above about 60° is overstated. Typical SHGC and Iac values are indicative centre-of-glass figures; manufacturer data replaces them.
