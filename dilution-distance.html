3. Flow and limit

The exhaust flow is the air leaving the exhaust opening; a larger flow needs more separation. Formula (C.1) works out the dilution factor fDil that reaches the intake from the flow and the distances. The limit it is checked against is not part of the formula: C.1.3.3 asks for the maximum dilution factor required to be specified, and sets fDil = 0.02 when none is. Change it only when the project brief or another standard gives a value, and record the source.

4. Result

The dilution factor is the share that actually arrives, from the flow and the distances you entered. It passes when that share is at or below the limit. Minimum l is the outside length you still need at the height you entered. Minimum Δh is the height gap you still need at the length you entered. A zero means that one dimension is already enough on its own. In situations 4 and 16 a higher exhaust-to-intake gap makes dilution worse, so the sheet gives the maximum Δh instead. In situation 7, l and Δh move together (l = Δh × √2), so the minimums are given as a pair.

Notes and formulae

    Formulae (C.1), (C.2) and (C.3) of BS EN 16798-3:2025, with C1 and C2 from Table C.3 for the situation chosen. The sheet checks (C.1) against the limit and uses (C.2) and (C.3) to give the minimum l and Δh.

    Worked with the values on the sheet, in the order the sheet calculates them. The figures update as you type.

    Method

    Formulae (C.1), (C.2) and (C.3) of BS EN 16798-3:2025. qv is in dm³/s. If no limit is specified, fDil = 0.02.

    Why 0.02

    C.1.3.3 says: "Specify the maximal dilution factor required (if not specified fDil = 0,02)". The standard gives no reason for the value; it is a default to use when the project does not specify a limit.

    Coefficients

    C1 and C2 are those of Table C.3, taken there from NEN 1087, which uses the same relation up to 1 m³/s.

    Existing buildings

    Situations 9, 10 and 12 are not for new buildings.

    Connecting distance l

    Where l is worked out from measurements, it is the shortest line over the outside surfaces: l = √(x² + Δh²) on one wall, l = √((a + b)² + Δh²) round an outside corner, and l = Δh × √2 in situation 7.

    Larger systems

    Applies only up to 0.5 m³/s. C.1.3.3 covers exhaust air volume flows typically ≤ 0.5 m³/s (500 dm³/s); the flow that counts is the greater of the exhaust and the outdoor-air flow. For larger flows, C.1.3.2 sends you to Figure C.1 (sheet VIII), and the results of this sheet should not be used.
