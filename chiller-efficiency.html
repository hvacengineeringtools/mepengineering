Notes and formulae

Adjustment, §6.4.1.2.1

    Part-load value and the maker’s selection

      Section 2 scales the maker’s COP and IPLV by the ratio of the 90.1 factors at your conditions and at the maker’s conditions. 90.1 applies Kadj to the IPLV requirement in the same way, so an adjusted IPLV is the standard’s NPLV. The factor is a curve for the minimum requirement; it is not the maker’s data for the unit, and it ignores any change in capacity, flow or compressor limits. Ask the maker for a selection at the new conditions and enter it in step 3 to see the difference.

      The calculation step by step, with the values now on the sheet. The figures update as you type.

      What each 90.1-2025 (SI) table assumes when it states a minimum efficiency: the chilled water, the heat-rejection side, the outdoor air and the part-load points. “Standard” conditions are those of AHRI 551/591 (SI), which Table 6.8.1-3 and 6.8.1-16 refer to. The tables in 90.1 give the requirement; the conditions behind IPLV.SI are in AHRI 551/591 (SI) Tables 4 and 6, not in 90.1 itself.

      Chillers, Tables 6.8.1-3 and 6.8.1-16

      Full-load rating: 7.00 °C leaving and 12.00 °C entering chilled water (§6.4.1.2.1). Water-cooled condenser: 30.00 °C entering and 35.00 °C leaving. Air-cooled: 35.0 °C entering dry-bulb. IPLV.SI = 0.01A + 0.42B + 0.45C + 0.12D, where A, B, C, D are the COPs at 100, 75, 50 and 25 % load. In AHRI 551/591 the condenser water flow is held at its full-load value at every part-load point, and the chilled water leaves at 7.00 °C throughout.

      Part-load points of the IPLV.SI rating

      At part load the heat-rejection temperature falls with the load, which is why IPLV.SI is higher than the full-load COP. Water-cooled units are rated with condenser water falling from 30.0 to 19.0 °C (it stops falling at 19.0 °C between 50 and 25 % load); air-cooled units with ambient falling from 35.0 to 13.0 °C. A plant whose tower or ambient does not follow that pattern will not see the IPLV.SI in service.

      Heat-rejection equipment

      The tower’s own rating conditions (Table 6.8.1-7: 35.0 °C in, 29.4 °C out, 23.9 °C wet-bulb for an open-circuit tower, a range of 5.6 K and an approach of 5.5 K) are on sheet XV. The chiller standard condition of 30.00 / 35.00 °C is a range of 5.0 K, and its 30.00 °C entering water is 0.6 K warmer than the tower’s rating outlet. The two ratings are separate. Your site’s wet-bulb, tower approach and chiller lift decide the real conditions.

      What distinguishes the unit types

      • Air-cooled: rated on outdoor dry-bulb (35.0 °C). The condenser leaving temperature does not exist as a water condition, so no Kadj applies. Ambient is the variable that changes performance.
      • Liquid-cooled, positive displacement and absorption: rated at 30.00 / 35.00 °C condenser water. No Kadj; other water temperatures need the maker’s data.
      • Liquid-cooled centrifugal: the same standard point, but 90.1 allows the requirement to be adjusted by Kadj for a design outside it, between 2.20 and 21.10 °C leaving evaporator water, 15.56 to 57.00 °C leaving condenser water and a lift of 11.00 to 44.00 K.
      • Heat pumps and heat recovery (Table 6.8.1-16): the cooling rating is the same as above. Heating has its own rating points: air-source 8.00 °C db / 6.00 °C wb and −8.00 °C db / −9.00 °C wb; liquid-source with the source water leaving at 7.00 °C (19.00 °C for the boost point), and heating water at 35/40, 42/50, 50/60 °C (low, medium, high) or 50/60 °C (boost).
      • Evaporatively cooled chillers: AHRI 551/591 rates them on 24.00 °C entering wet-bulb (part load 24.0, 20.5, 17.0, 13.5 °C). They are not a row in Table 6.8.1-3.

      Chiller

      §6.4.1.2.1: Kadj applies to liquid-cooled centrifugal chillers not designed for the AHRI 551/591 standard conditions (7.00 °C leaving and 12.00 °C entering evaporator water, 30.00 °C entering and 35.00 °C leaving condenser water). At exactly 7.00 and 35.00 °C the sheet applies no adjustment; the formula gives 0.9995. The adjusted values are valid only for LvgEvap 2.20 to 21.10 °C, LvgCond 15.56 to 57.00 °C and lift 11.00 to 44.00 K; outside them the standard does not cover the chiller. Other types in Table 6.8.1-3 have no adjustment: they are rated at the test standard’s conditions.

      The standard’s text calls the adjusted figures “maximum”; they are minimum COP values.

      Manufacturer’s selection (section 2)

      Enter the maker’s selection at its rated water temperatures and change “your conditions” in section 1. The sheet estimates the COP, input power and IPLV at your conditions from the 90.1 factor, shows the change, and checks both the maker’s figures and the estimate against the 90.1 minimum at the corresponding conditions. The matrix underneath gives the estimated COP for a range of condenser and evaporator leaving temperatures. Only the leaving temperatures enter Kadj; the entering temperatures give the temperature differences and the chilled-water flow. The factor applies to liquid-cooled centrifugal units; for other types, enter the maker’s own figures at your conditions in step 3.

      Unit already selected

      If the maker’s COP is quoted at the same water temperatures as the new conditions, compare it directly with the adjusted requirement. If the conditions changed after the unit was rated, the sheet estimates the unit’s new COP as COP × Kadj(new) ÷ Kadj(rated). Kadj is a curve for the minimum requirement and is not the maker’s data for the unit, so treat that as a guide.

      Points to check in the standard’s tables

      Table 6.8.1-3 gives the last positive-displacement size as “≥2100 kW” while the row above ends at 2110 kW; the sheet uses 2110 kW. Table 6.8.1-16 prints the IPLV.SI for liquid-source positive displacement, 1055 to 2110 kW, as “≤6.432” and “≤8.157”; every other row is “≥”, so the sheet treats them as minimums. The first liquid-source centrifugal row prints “<39.57 and <528”; the sheet reads it as 39.57 to 528 kW (note q).
