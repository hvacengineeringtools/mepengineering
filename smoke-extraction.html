1. The sizing rules at a glance

BS 9991 sets free areas and air change rates for most cases. For a mechanical system serving a lobby or corridor it gives no flow rate: the rate is to be set by CFD analysis or calculation with a sensitivity study (Annex A.4). The last calculator tab therefore works a starting rate out in four steps and checks the rest.

                    What is from BS 9991 and what is not

                    • From BS 9991:2015: the free areas of vents (1 m², 1.5 m², 0.4 m²), the natural shaft dimensions and heights, the basement outlet area of 2.5 % of the floor area, the ten air changes per hour, 300 °C for 60 minutes and 600 °C ductwork for powered basement extract, the 100 N door force limit, and the exemptions for small basements.
                    • Not from BS 9991: the SCA design fires and the 2 m³/s good-practice minimum, the hinged vent free area (a geometric estimate in the manner of Approved Document B, Diagram C.7, which the standard points to; use the tested aerodynamic free area of the vent to BS EN 12101-2 for design), the door force formula (the form used in BS EN 12101-6 and BS 5588-4), the stack flow, and the duct, shaft and opening velocities, which are your choices.
                    • For a mechanical system serving a lobby or corridor, BS 9991 gives no flow. It asks for conditions at least equal to the natural system (14.2.4) and leaves the rate to CFD or calculation (A.4). The sheet therefore works out a starting rate; see “How the lobby or corridor rate is worked out” below.
                    • A mechanical lobby or corridor shaft follows 14.2.3.2 a), f), g), i) and j) only (14.2.4). The 1.5 m² shaft area and the 2.5 m height rule belong to the natural shaft and are not applied to it.

                    How the lobby or corridor rate is worked out

                    • A, smoke from the flat. The extract must at least keep pace with the smoke coming out of the flat. The SCA guidance (Oct 2015, section 5.3.4 and 5.3.5) gives design fires for the flat: 250 kW (0.2 kg/s of smoke at 210 °C), 1000 kW (0.9 kg/s at 360 °C) and 2500 kW (1.4 kg/s at 690 °C) in steady state, and 1000 kW at the open flat door in the time-dependent case (1.0 kg/s at 120 °C). The sheet divides the mass flow by the air density at the extract temperature (ρ = 353/(T + 273.15)) to get a volume flow.
                    • B, natural system benchmark. BS 9991 14.2.4 requires conditions at least equal to the natural system the mechanical one replaces. The sheet estimates the flow the compliant natural vents would give by stack effect (corridor vent 1.5 m² and stair-head vent 1.0 m² in series). It is a benchmark, not a compliance route: only CFD shows equivalence.
                    • C, small single-stair building. For mechanical ventilation in such buildings the SCA guidance (6.5.4) gives the higher of 10 air changes of the stair and 2 m³/s, as good practice, which the designer may change with the approval of the authority.
                    • D. The sheet takes the largest of A, B and C as a starting rate, or your own figure from the CFD.
                    • E to G. The extracted air has to be replaced. The pressure the replacement opening causes follows from the flow and its free area (ΔP = ½ρ(Q/CdA)²). The SCA guidance suggests no more than 20 Pa across lobby doors, and the pressure must keep the door opening force at the handle to 100 N or less (BS 9991 14.2.4, SCA 5.3.1).
                    • Quoted figures not used. A surveyor’s guide (Group SCS) quotes 2.5 m³/s for means of escape and 5.0 m³/s for fire-fighting. I could not find these in the SCA 2015 text, so they are shown as a reference only. Fire-fighting needs a boost rate above the escape rate.
                    • The SCA figures are as read from the October 2015 edition; the current edition is Revision 3.1 (2020). Check them against your copy.

                    Not covered

                    • Pressure differential (pressurization) systems, BS EN 12101-6. Car park smoke ventilation (BS 7346-7 and Approved Document B). Atria (Annex C). Commercial buildings (BS 9999).
                    • Wind effects on natural vents, fire-fighting shaft pressure, travel distances in extended corridors (Figures 6 and 7).
                    • Replacement air routes, fire dampers, fan curves and duct pressure drops.
