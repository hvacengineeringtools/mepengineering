Lighting sizing. Two methods for a room. The zonal cavity method finds how many luminaires give an average maintained illuminance on the working plane, using the cavity ratios, the effective cavity reflectances, the coefficient of utilisation (CU) from the maker’s table and the light loss factor. The point-by-point method then finds the illuminance at any point from the intensity of each luminaire, by the inverse square and cosine law, and draws the room as a plan so you can see the uniformity. Fill in sections 1 and 2 for the number of luminaires; add section 3 to check the spread of light. The CU table comes from the luminaire maker’s data sheet: this sheet does not hold luminaire data.

2. Zonal cavity method (average illuminance and number of luminaires)

The room is split into three cavities: the ceiling cavity above the luminaires, the room cavity between the luminaires and the working plane, and the floor cavity below the working plane. Each has a cavity ratio. The ceiling and floor cavities are given an effective reflectance, which is what the CU table needs.

3. Point-by-point method (illuminance at each point, and the spread)

Each luminaire is treated as a point source. At a point on the working plane, the illuminance from one luminaire is E = I(θ) · cos θ ÷ d². The sheet adds the luminaires, multiplies by the light loss factor, and works out the figures at points spread over the room. This is the direct light only: it leaves out light reflected from the ceiling and walls, which adds to what you see in practice.

          Which method for what

          • Zonal cavity method. It gives the average illuminance on the working plane and the number of luminaires. It includes the light reflected by the ceiling, walls and floor, through the CU. It tells you nothing about how even the light is.
          • Point-by-point method. It gives the illuminance at a chosen point from each luminaire’s intensity. It is used for the spread of light, for a task at a particular place, and for narrow beams such as downlights and spotlights, where the average is a poor guide. It counts direct light only.
          • Together. Size with the zonal cavity method, then check the layout with the point-by-point method. The point-by-point average is lower than the zonal average, because the reflected light is left out. A direct-only average of about 70 to 90 % of the zonal figure is usual for a light-coloured room.

          Reading the CU table

          • The maker’s table is entered by the room cavity ratio and by the effective ceiling cavity reflectance and the wall reflectance, for a floor cavity reflectance of 20 %. The sheet shows the column to read, and the CU you enter is for that column.
          • Type the CU for each room cavity ratio in the table as pairs, for example 1:0.88, 2:0.82, 3:0.76. The sheet interpolates between them for your room.
          • If your room cavity ratio is beyond the table, the sheet uses the nearest end value and warns you. That is a long, narrow room or one with a high ceiling, and the result is a guess.
          • The examples use an illustrative table. A real design needs the table for the luminaire chosen.

          Limits of the methods

          • Zonal cavity. It assumes luminaires spread evenly over the ceiling and a room with no obstructions. A row of luminaires, a very uneven layout, or tall racking breaks the assumption.
          • Point by point. It treats each luminaire as a point. That is good when the distance to the point is at least five times the luminaire size. Very close, or with long linear luminaires, the error is large.
          • Daylight is not included. Windows can reduce the electric light needed, and the room can be too dim away from them.
          • Glare, colour rendering, flicker and emergency lighting are not calculated. Check the unified glare rating from the maker’s table, and the emergency lighting separately.
          • Energy. The sheet reports the installed power in W/m² and per 100 lux. Compare it with the limit in the energy code used on the project.

          Sources

          The zonal cavity method follows the method of the IES Lighting Handbook (cavity ratios, effective cavity reflectances, coefficient of utilisation, light loss factor). The point-by-point method is the inverse square and cosine law. Target illuminances follow EN 12464-1 and CIBSE guidance. Check results against the licensed documents.
