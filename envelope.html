    Notes and formulae

    Abbreviations & units

    Values in the tables are the assembly maximum (U, C or F) and the insulation minimum (R). Lower U / C / F is better; higher R is better.

    U-factor (U-value)
    How easily heat passes through a whole assembly (wall, roof, window, etc.).
    It is the rate of heat flow through 1 m² of the assembly when the temperature difference across it is 1 K.
    A lower U-factor means less heat is lost (or gained).
    Unit: W/(m²·K)
    R-value
    How well a material or layer resists the flow of heat. It is the opposite of U-factor (R = 1/U for a simple layer).
    Think of it as the temperature difference (in K) needed to drive 1 W of heat through 1 m² of the insulation.
    A higher R-value means better insulation.
    Unit: m²·K/W
    C-factor (C-value)
    Used for below-grade walls. It plays the same role as U-factor but for the conductance of the underground wall assembly.
    It is the rate of heat flow through 1 m² of the below-grade wall when the temperature difference is 1 K.
    Unit: W/(m²·K)
    F-factor (F-value)
    Used for slab-on-grade floors. It describes heat loss at the edge of the slab (where it meets the outdoor air or ground).
    It is the rate of heat flow per metre of slab perimeter when the temperature difference is 1 K.
    Unit: W/(m·K)
    SHGC
    Solar Heat Gain Coefficient of a window or skylight.
    The fraction of solar radiation that passes through the glazing and becomes heat inside the building (0 = none, 1 = all).
    Dimensionless (0–1)
    NR
    No requirement. The standard does not set a maximum U/C/F or a minimum R for that element in that space type and climate zone.
    c.i.
    Continuous insulation. Insulation that runs continuously across structural members so there are no thermal bridges (except fasteners and service openings).
    FC
    Filled cavity. Insulation that completely fills the cavity of a metal-building roof or wall.
    Ls
    Liner system. A layer of insulation installed as a liner (commonly in metal buildings), often used together with other insulation.
    Nonresidential / Residential / Semiheated
    Space categories used in the 90.1 envelope tables. The same climate zone can have different U/R limits depending on which category the space falls into.
