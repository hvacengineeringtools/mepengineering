Adds up the pressure loss along one circuit, the index run, from the pump round to the pump. Each section is either a length of pipe with its fittings, or an item of equipment with the pressure drop from the maker’s data. Every pipe section uses the same Darcy–Weisbach and Altshul–Tsal friction as sheet VI, with the same pipe tables, so the size you choose here is the size sheet VI would give you. The running total is kept as you go, and the whole table can be downloaded as an Excel workbook that recalculates, or printed for submission.

4. Checking the K values

How far our reference K values are from a published method. The Darby 3-K method gives K for a fitting from its diameter and the Reynolds number, so it needs no table of sizes. Pick a fitting and a Reynolds number to see the two side by side, and see what the difference does to the circuit you have entered.

About 100,000 is typical for chilled water at 1 to 1.5 m/s in DN100.

Effect on the circuit above

What the comparison shows (Reynolds number 100,000)

  • Our reference table: the table has no separate 125 mm row of its own; the 125 mm values repeat the 100 mm ones.
  • 90° elbow and tee (run through): within about 20 % of the 3-K method from DN25 to DN50. Higher at DN15 and DN20, up to 36 % higher. From DN65 upward the table is lower and the gap grows with size: about 30 % low at DN80, 35 % at DN100, and 45 to 55 % low at DN150 to DN300.
  • Tee, branch and 45° elbow: lower than 3-K at every size, by 20 to 40 %.
  • Gate valve: three to four times the 3-K value at DN15 to DN25, close from DN100 to DN150, about half at DN300. Angle valve: three to four times at DN15 and DN20, within 15 % from DN40. Globe valve: 10 to 95 % higher than 3-K. Swing check valve: a constant 2.0, which is up to 25 % low in small sizes and 20 to 50 % high from DN100.
  • What it means: the table falls with size faster than the 3-K method does. In the larger sizes, where most of the pressure loss of a plant room is, the table gives lower fitting losses than a published method, so it may be on the low side. I cannot say which of the two matches the actual Hattersley fittings, and the 3-K values here are for standard, not long-radius, elbows. Valves are best entered from the maker’s Kv or Cv data as equipment.
Preliminary checking only. Straight pipe friction and fittings are by the standard K × velocity pressure method; the K values and the equipment figures are yours to check against the maker and the design guide. Notes and formulae

The working for the section you last changed, with the values now on the sheet.

1. Velocity and Reynolds number

v = Q / A, with A = π D² / 4 and Q in m³/s (litres per second ÷ 1000).
Re = v D / ν

2. Friction factor (Altshul–Tsal)

f′ = 0.11 (ε / D + 68 / Re)^0.25
f = f′ if f′ ≥ 0.018, otherwise f = 0.85 f′ + 0.0028

3. Pipe friction

Δp per metre = (f / D) × ½ ρ v²
Pipe loss = Δp per metre × length

4. Fittings

Σ K = Σ (number of each fitting × its K at the size) + any extra K you enter
Fittings loss = Σ K × ½ ρ v²

5. Equipment

Pressure drop from the maker’s data, entered in kPa. At a higher flow it is scaled by (flow ratio)² only if the section is marked “varies with flow²”.

6. Running total and design total

Section = pipe + fittings + equipment
Running total = sum of the sections down to this one
Subtotal = (pipe + fittings) × 1 or 2 + equipment
Design total = subtotal × (1 + margin)
Head of water = design total × 1000 / (ρ g)

Velocity and friction flags

A section is flagged when the velocity is above 1.5 m/s up to DN50 or 3 m/s above, or when the pressure drop per metre is above 250 Pa/m up to DN20, 180 Pa/m for DN25 to DN50 and 300 Pa/m from DN65. These come from our pipe-sizing reference data. They are flags only: the total still counts the section.

How this sheet works

  • One row per pipe, fitting or equipment item, with the running total and a design margin. The friction formula is the same as sheet VI.
  • Density and viscosity follow the water temperature you enter.
  • The workbook this sheet writes uses ordinary formulas, so it opens and recalculates without macros.

Choosing the K for a size

The K table is by nominal size. For a size that is not in it (22 mm copper, for example), the sheet uses the next smaller size in the table, which has the larger K, and above 300 it uses 300. The size used is shown on the section and you can change it.

Points to know about the K table

  • The 125 mm row repeats the 100 mm values for every fitting except the expansion and the Y-strainer. Check it if you use 125 mm fittings.
  • The Y-strainer K (about 4) is a placeholder for a typical strainer. For a real strainer enter the maker’s pressure drop as equipment.
  • The “expansion” item is not defined further in the reference data; it may be a bellows, a loop or a sudden enlargement.

What is not included

Static head, pump head and curve, control valve authority, the effect of glycol, ageing of the pipe, and the extra loss in tees where the flow splits (use the tee and branch counts for the section that carries the flow you are following).
