Notes and formulae

Worked with the values on the sheet. The figures update as you type.

Obstruction levels

Pick the one level that best describes the space as a whole, from the inlet to the extract. K is the pressure loss of the obstructions as a multiple of the dynamic pressure ½ρv², at the mean air speed in the full cross-section. The sheet adds 0.5 for the inlet and 1.0 for the discharge.

LevelK range (top used)What it looks likeAbout this share of the cross-section blocked

The levels and K ranges are a design allowance; no published table of them was found. The last column checks them against the loss of a thin grid of sharp-edged openings (Fried and Idelchik, Diagram 8-1), K = (1 + 0.707 √(1 − f) − f)² / f², where f is the open share. Use it as a guide: look along the space and judge roughly how much of the cross-section the obstructions block.

Choosing the level

  • Where the space is not the same throughout, choose the level of the most obstructed part, or use Custom K.
  • In a very long space, a higher K within the range is more appropriate than in a short one.
  • The sheet uses the top of each range, to be on the safe side. Where air can flow freely over the top of the obstructions, the real loss is lower and you may type a smaller K.
  • Inlet: K 0.5 sharp-edged, 0.1 bellmouthed. Discharge with no recovery: K 1.0 (PIARC). Louvres or screens in the path: add the maker’s coefficient, or use sheet XI.

How accurate the coefficients are

Worked example: an obstructed hall

A hand calculation for a hall 100 × 24 × 6 m, checked with this sheet. Inputs: Q = 23.0588 m³/s, ρ = 1.225 kg/m³, K = 20 in total (18.5 obstruction + 1.5 inlet and discharge), 91 N at η = 0.5, 3.08 m³/s, Ø400, terminal velocity 0.3 m/s, Kv = 1, no overlap, 12 m between fan lines, no extract fan and no louvres. The link above the inputs loads the same case.

What the check shows

  1. The flow per fan is Qj × ER, not Qj × (1 + ER). 3.08 × 4.104 = 12.64 m³/s. The entrainment law gives the total flow in the jet at distance x as a multiple of the fan’s own flow, so ER is not added to 1.
  2. The reach uses Kv = 1. 24.62 × 0.4 ÷ 0.3 = 32.83 m. A free round jet decays with Kv ≈ 6, so the hand calculation allows for a jet that dies away six times faster, a heavy allowance for congestion. Record the reason for the value chosen.
  3. Why a maker’s program may give 6. 100 m ÷ 32.83 m = 3.05 rows along the hall, rounded up to 4. A reach of 33.4 m, only 2 % longer, gives 3 × 2 = 6. The two answers sit either side of a step; the maker’s measured throw is very likely longer than the Kv = 1 estimate. Ask the maker for the throw to 0.3 m/s and enter it as Maker’s throw.
  4. Momentum alone needs only 1 or 2 fans. 0.31 Pa × 144 m² ≈ 45 N, met by one fan at 45.5 N effective. The count is set by throw and lateral spacing (Method 1), not by the obstruction loss; K matters less than it seems unless the hall is very congested.

How jet fans ventilate a space

The extract fans at one end take out the airflow the space needs; make-up air comes in at the other end. On its own, that air would take the easiest path and leave corners and bays stagnant. Jet fans fix this. Each one blows a fast jet of air, about 20 m/s at the fan, along the ceiling. The jet slows and widens as it drags the surrounding air with it, and hands that air on to the next fan downstream.

The two methods

Method 1 · throw and coverage. Rows sit one throw apart along the airflow, less an overlap; fan lines sit no more than the largest lateral spacing apart across it. This sets where the fans must be so that every part of the space is swept. A maker’s rule of thumb of thrust per floor area is shown alongside as a check.

Method 2 · pressure loss and induced airflow. Two further tests for a space that is full of equipment:

  1. Momentum. The obstructions resist the airflow with Δp = Ktotal × ½ρv². The fans together must push at least Δp × A, each giving η × T × (1 − v / vj).
  2. Airflow carried. A free jet carries 0.32 × x / D times its own flow at distance x (Ricou and Spalding). In a room it cannot carry more than its share of the cross-section moving at the terminal velocity. The fan lines together must carry at least Q.
  3. Jet fans alone. With no extract fan, the fans must push Q through the obstructions and the openings and against wind and stack pressure. The sheet also solves for the airflow the installed fans actually drive. Opening losses are converted to the hall speed: K (A / Aopening)², so small openings dominate.

The layout takes the rows from Method 1 and adds fan lines until every test is met. The check that needs most fans is marked “sets the count”.

How close is the throw estimate? Compared with one maker’s published guidance, with a 1.0 m/s terminal velocity and 20% overlap: a 98 N fan gives about 49 m row spacing against the maker’s 50 m; a 28 N fan about 26 m against 30 m. Downstand beams, ramps, parked cars and equipment shorten real throws considerably.

Limits

This is a preliminary design. CFD simulation shall be done to verify the end result. In particular:

  • Jets will not follow a straight line through dense equipment. Dead zones behind obstructions and air short-circuiting over the top of them are only found by CFD.
  • Keep the jet clear of obstructions for the first 10 to 20 m: a jet that hits a beam or duct close to the fan loses much of its thrust.
  • A smoke control system, one that keeps a clear path for firefighters, needs CFD analysis to BS 7346-7. Truly reversible fans can have less thrust in reverse; use the maker’s figure. Check the fan’s temperature rating against the fire strategy.
  • Also check noise, electrical load, controls, structural support, maintenance access and safe isolation.
