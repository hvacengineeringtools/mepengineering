        What the sheet does

        The number of air changes per hour (ACH, or ACPH) is the volume of air moved into or out of the room in one hour divided by the volume of the room. The sheet turns that around: from the room volume and a chosen ACH it gives the flow. The rate for the room type comes from the table in the side bar; where the table gives a range you pick the low, middle or high end, or enter your own.

        Source and limits

        • The table is from “Recommended Air Changes per Hour, Air Change Rates by Room” (Axair Fans, correct as of December 2021), which quotes DIN 1946 part 2. The document says the rates depend on the size of the room and the number of occupants and should be used with the ACH formula. It calls the list non-exhaustive.
        • I corrected four spelling errors in the room names (entrance halls and corridors, supermarkets, foundries, chemical stores). Some rooms appear twice in the source under different names, such as paper mills; both are kept. The values are as printed.
        • The document’s second table, fresh air in m³/h per person by room usage, is blank in the copy supplied. The sheet therefore takes the per-person figure as an input rather than inventing one.
        • An air change rate is a rule of thumb. It does not by itself deal with heat gains, pollutant release, smoke, odour or fire safety. Kitchens, laboratories, car parks, plant rooms and anything with fumes need their own design criteria.
        • Mechanical extract for a basement smoke vent is a separate rule (10 air changes per hour with fans rated for 300 °C); see sheet XX.
        • The flow is taken as the supply or extract air flow, not the recirculated flow. The sheet does not check the air distribution, or whether the air reaches every part of the room.

        Source

        Axair Fans UK, “Recommended Air Changes per Hour” (December 2021), after DIN 1946 part 2.
