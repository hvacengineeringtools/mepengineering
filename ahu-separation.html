1. AHU roof unit — intake to exhaust

Table E.3 is for one supply-and-extract unit on the roof. A is the distance between its fresh-air opening and its used-air opening. Two or more separate units are not in this table: use sheet VIII.

Heat recovery classification

Two streams meet inside the heat recovery. A leak moves air from the tunnel at the higher pressure into the tunnel at the lower pressure.

What the terms mean
TermMeaning
Fresh-air tunnelOutside air passing through the heat recovery toward the rooms. The standard calls this the outdoor-air side. It is not extract.
Used-air tunnelAir from the rooms passing through the heat recovery toward the outside. The standard calls this the exhaust-air side.
ODA (P)Dust in the outside air, before the filters. 1 is the cleanest, 3 is the dustiest.
SUP (P)Dust allowed in the air to the rooms, after the filters. 1 is the cleanest. SUP 6 means no dust filter.
ODA (G)Gas in the outside air: nitrogen dioxide, sulphur dioxide or ozone. 1 is the cleanest. Leave this unused if you are only choosing a dust filter.
SUP (G)How much of that gas may remain in the air to the rooms. 1 is the cleanest. Not the same list as SUP (P).
ETAUsed air from the rooms. 1 is the cleanest, 4 is the dirtiest.
EATRMeasured on the heat recovery only. The percent of air toward the rooms that came from the used-air tunnel. Type 2 for 2%, not 0.02.
OACFMeasured on the heat recovery only. Fresh air entering it, divided by air leaving it toward the rooms. 1.00 means the two flows match. Its class need not match the EATR class.

2. Must the tunnels be kept apart?

ETA 1 may go back to other rooms. ETA 2 may go back only to the same room. ETA 3 and ETA 4 do not go back to any room.
