1. Arrangement

With the room warmer than outside, air leaves through the high openings A1 and A2 and enters through the low openings A3 and A4. ha is the height between the high and low centre lines.

2. Formula

Qb = Cd Ab (2 Δθ ha g / (θ̄ + 273.15))0.5
1 / Ab² = 1 / (A1 + A2)² + 1 / (A3 + A4)²

For stack flow the high openings act together, the low openings act together, and the two groups are in series: A1 + A2 is the total outlet and A3 + A4 the total inlet. BS 5925 writes the same rule with its own opening numbers, in which its two upper openings are A1 and A3. Cd defaults to 0.61. θ̄ is the mean temperature in °C.

3. Windows in the openings

The areas entered below are the free areas of the holes in the wall. Where a hole is closed by a window that opens only part way, less air gets through. For each opening, choose the window. The sheet multiplies the area by the factor F and puts the effective area F × A into the formula. The openings act in series, so the most restricted one limits the flow. Only referenced factors are offered: J(φ) from BS 5925 and the window maker’s tested data.

How to evaluate an opening window
  1. Maker’s tested data, best. If the window maker gives an effective or aerodynamic free area at your opening angle, tested for example to BS EN 13141-1, divide it by Cd × A and enter the result as Maker’s factor.
  2. Casement or centre-pivoted light: J(φ). Read J(φ) from figs. 3a and 3b, the same curves as sheet I. Here it is used as a reduction on the area of that opening and capped at 1. BS 5925 gives J(φ) for one opening with buoyancy, so applying it to each opening of a pair is an approximation.
  3. Top-, bottom- or side-hung sash. BS 5925 gives no factor for these. Use the maker’s tested effective area at the opening angle, entered as Maker’s factor.
  4. Louvre, grille or open hole. Choose No window and enter the free area. Use sheet XI for a louvre’s own pressure loss.

Figs. 3a and 3b are the curves of sheet I, read from the opening-light charts of BS 5925:1991 at 0, 15, 30, 45, 60 and 90° and joined with straight lines. Between the three height ÷ width curves the sheet interpolates; outside 0.86–2.28 it uses the nearest curve. Check them against your copy of the standard.

4. Values

Enter numeric areas and height. F × A1 + F × A2 and F × A3 + F × A4 must both be greater than zero.

5. Temperature sweep

Indoor temperature held still; outdoor temperature moves ±10 °C.

Method: BS 5925:1991, buoyancy-driven flow through openings on opposite walls. High openings act in series with low openings: 1/Ab² = 1/(A1 + A2)² + 1/(A3 + A4)². Wind is not included. Each area is multiplied by its window factor F first: J(φ) from the BS 5925 opening-light charts (capped at 1, an approximation outside the single-opening case), or a maker’s figure. g = 9.81 m/s²; mean absolute temperature θ̄ + 273.15 K with θ̄ = (Ti + To)/2 in °C. Default Cd = 0.61. The temperature sweep holds Ti and varies To from Ti − 10 K to Ti + 10 K.
